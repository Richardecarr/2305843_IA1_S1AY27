7 Cures for a Lean Purse

The first of the cures, "Start thy purse to fattening", is the idea that I should always set aside a part of the money which I earn rather than spend all of it. When I was a student I would put aside at least 10 per cent of any money that I received, no matter whether it was from working, a side job or any other source.

The advice known as the second cure,' Control thy expenditures,' serves to remind me of the importance of managing my spending and of distinguishing between my needs and my wants. I can put this into practice by drawing up a simple budget and by refraining from unnecessary spending, in particular when it comes to things which I do not really need.

The third remedy, 'Make thy gold multiply', implies that my savings should eventually act on my behalf. To put this into practice I would need to gain some knowledge about investing and invest a portion of my savings in investments which can grow over time.

The fourth remedy, "Guard your treasures from loss," tells me not to take unnecessary risks with my money; before making an investment I carefully carry out some research and ensure that I understand what I am putting my money into.

The fifth remedy, "Make your dwelling a profitable investment", emphasizes the importance of owning an asset which can give me financial benefit. I intend, in the future, to own some property which could offer stability and might become an additional source of income.

The sixth remedy, 'Insure a future income', makes me aware of the need to plan for the future rather than concentrating solely on my present circumstances. I should therefore create some savings and investments which will be able to support me in later life and give security to my family.

In short, the idea of 'increasing your ability to earn' is that I should keep on improving myself; and since I am a student of Computer Science, I can do this by enhancing my programming skills, gaining some experience, and learning new technologies in order that I may be qualified for better opportunities.

In fact, the seven cures have shown me that it is not about getting rich quickly if you want to build up your wealth; rather, it is a matter of forming good habits, making wise decisions regarding your money, investing in yourself, and staying consistent over time.

5 Laws of Gold

The first law of gold is to save some, at least, of all that I earn. As a student, I may not be making a lot of money right now, but I can start small and save 10% of my income and build the habit for the future.

The second law teaches me that money should not just sit there, it should be put to work. I can do this by learning about investing and eventually investing some of my savings into investments that will grow over time.

The third law reminds me to protect my money by being careful with my financial decisions. ‘Before I invest, I’d rather do my homework and talk to people who actually know and have experience, rather than just following the crowd.

The fourth law tells me not to put my money into things I don’t understand. That is what I would do. I would take the time to learn about an investment before I put my money in it. I'd much rather turn down an opportunity than lose money because I didn't know what I was doing.

The fifth law is a reminder to be on guard for promises of quick and impractical riches. In my own life, I want to stay away from scams, get-rich-quick schemes, and investments that seem too good to be true. I'd rather grow my wealth slowly and steadily.

Overall, the five laws taught me that financial success is not about getting rich quick. It’s about saving every month, making my money work for me, protecting what I have, learning and making smart decisions. These are habits I can start building today that I can take with me for the rest of my life.

My biggest takeaway

The key lesson that I have learnt from both The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse and The Five Laws of Gold is that attaining wealth mainly comes down to discipline and possessing good habits rather than simply earning a large sum of money; I have come to the conclusion that I should save a portion of whatever I earn, maintain strict control over my spending, get my money to work for me, protect it, and at the same time continue to improve my skills so as to be able to earn more.

I am a student who wishes to establish a career in Computer Science, so at this stage I can start putting these lessons into practice even though my income is not large. The important thing is to begin on a small scale, remain consistent, learn from my mistakes, and improve my financial decisions as I go on.
